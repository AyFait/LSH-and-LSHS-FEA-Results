i.	Strain Evaluation: The equivalent elastic strain (Figure 14a) and maximum principal elastic strain (Figure 14b) correspond linearly with the stress distributions. Peak elastic strain concentrated at the contact point of the bolt and the LSHS was bounded at 0.00168mm/mm, indicating negligible microscopic structural distortion across the components.

ii.	Stress Distribution: The equivalent (von-Mises) stress distribution profiles (Figure 15a) reveal that structural loading is smoothly transferred through the contact interfaces. The maximum localized von-Mises stress recorded within the structural body of the LSHS assembly reached 141.51MPa, concentrated naturally around regions of geometric transition and contact pressure. Crucially, the broad bulk of the LSHS structure maintains low, nominal stress levels well below this localized peak value.  
The stress fields indicate that as the displacement load is enforced, the contact pressures are distributed effectively through the curved interface of the LSHS. The absence of severe, wide-area stress concentrations confirms that the geometric design avoids sharp stress-riser anomalies. Because the peak equivalent stress is localized to immediate contact boundaries, the structural integrity of the component's core remains completely uncompromised.
Furthermore, the maximum principal stress plots (Figure 15b) demonstrate that tensile stress states remain well within safe limits. This uniform distribution of tensile and compressive regions implies that the LSH and LSHS assembly possesses excellent structural stability, resisting micro-slip and ensuring an even clamping force distribution onto the enclosed cylindrical sieve.
